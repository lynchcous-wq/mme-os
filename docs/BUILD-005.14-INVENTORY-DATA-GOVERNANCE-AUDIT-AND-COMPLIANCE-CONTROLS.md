# Build 005.14 — Inventory Data Governance, Audit, and Compliance Controls

**Status:** Governance and control design only  
**Branch:** implementation/build-005-inventory-core  
**Scope:** Defines data classification, retention, audit evidence, access governance, privacy safeguards, and compliance operating controls for Inventory Core. No policies, database objects, access rules, retention jobs, legal certifications, tests, or deployments are implemented by this document.

## 1. Purpose

Protect the accuracy, confidentiality, traceability, and appropriate use of inventory records across MME OS. Governance must support day-to-day retail operations while preserving evidence for disputes, loss investigations, returns, financial reconciliation, and management review.

This specification is an internal system-design control framework, not a legal opinion or a claim that MME is certified or compliant with any particular law or standard.

## 2. Governance principles

1. Inventory records have an accountable business owner.
2. Every stock-affecting action has a traceable actor or trusted service identity, timestamp, reason, and correlation reference.
3. The movement ledger is append-only in normal application operation; corrections are compensating entries, not destructive rewrites.
4. Access is role- and location-scoped, least-privilege, and reviewed periodically.
5. Sensitive identifiers are collected only when operationally necessary and are masked in ordinary interfaces.
6. Retention periods are approved by management with legal/accounting advice where required.
7. Audit records are protected from ordinary users and operational workflows.
8. Reports and exports are purpose-limited, access-controlled, and traceable.
9. A legal hold or investigation hold overrides routine deletion schedules where applicable.
10. No dashboard badge or policy document is proof of compliance without implemented controls and evidence.

## 3. Data classification

| Classification | Examples | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Approved product marketing attributes, public SKU description | May be displayed publicly after Catalog approval |
| Internal | SKU references, location codes, routine stock counts, workflow statuses | Authenticated access; scope by job role and location |
| Confidential | Unit acquisition cost, landed-cost allocation, supplier terms, shrink investigations, internal valuation | Restricted roles; log privileged access and exports |
| Restricted identifier | Serial number, IMEI, device activation/lock evidence, ownership/return evidence | Mask by default; reveal only to authorized roles for a documented purpose |
| Security-sensitive | Service credentials, tokens, signing secrets, privileged audit configuration | Never store in inventory business records or expose in logs, browser payloads, or exports |

Classification attaches to fields and derived reports, not merely to an entire table. A report inherits the highest classification of the data it contains.

## 4. Data ownership and stewardship

Proposed accountability:
- **Inventory Core owner:** inventory state, movements, reservations, holds, locations, and stock reconciliation.
- **Catalog owner:** product descriptions, marketing attributes, and SKU master data.
- **Acquisition/COGS owner:** purchase evidence and item-level landed-cost calculation.
- **Pricing owner:** retail price, markdown policy, and price history.
- **Returns owner:** return eligibility, inspection decision, and disposition evidence.
- **Identity/HR owner:** person, employee relationship, role, and employment status.
- **Security/IT owner:** privileged access, security events, incident response, and system credentials.
- **Executive sponsor:** approves policy, risk acceptance, and retention schedule.

Each dataset must have a named accountable owner before production use. Delegated stewardship does not transfer source-of-truth ownership.

## 5. Audit evidence requirements

For each stock-affecting command, preserve as appropriate:
- immutable event/movement reference;
- entity and location references;
- action and before/after state references;
- actor or service principal reference;
- server-generated timestamp;
- reason code and human-readable explanation when required;
- approval reference for controlled actions;
- correlation and idempotency references;
- source workflow reference (receipt, order, return, transfer, count, or case);
- outcome and safe error classification.

Do not record raw secrets, payment credentials, or unnecessary personal information in audit evidence. Protected serial/IMEI values should be referenced through restricted records rather than copied into broad audit messages.

Audit timestamps must be generated by trusted server infrastructure and stored consistently (UTC recommended), with local display conversion at the UI boundary.

## 6. Access governance

### 6.1 Access model
- Resolve users through Build 004 identity.
- Grant inventory permissions through explicit server-side permission checks.
- Scope access by store/location and task responsibility.
- Separate read, create, receive, inspect, reserve, transfer, adjust, approve, export, and administer capabilities.
- Separate requestor and approver for high-risk adjustments and write-offs where practical.
- Do not treat job title, UI visibility, client metadata, or profile color as authorization.

### 6.2 Privileged access
Privileged actions include:
- viewing full serial/IMEI;
- viewing acquisition cost and valuation;
- approving adjustments or write-offs;
- bulk export;
- changing inventory policy or location controls;
- replaying failed integration events;
- changing retention or audit settings.

Require a specific permission, reason where appropriate, audit evidence, and periodic access review.

### 6.3 Access review
Proposed review cadence:
- at onboarding and role/location change;
- immediately on termination or access suspension;
- periodic manager review (recommended quarterly);
- after a security incident or suspected misuse.

Build 004 application-side session and actor integration remains unverified in the current project state. These controls cannot be considered operational until identity integration and server-side authorization are implemented and tested.

## 7. Privacy and identifier protection

- Collect serial/IMEI only when required for identification, warranty, anti-fraud, return, or service operations.
- Mask identifiers in lists, dashboards, notifications, exports, and ordinary search results.
- Require a documented permission to reveal the full value.
- Avoid placing identifiers in URLs, analytics events, browser logs, or support screenshots.
- Do not use serial/IMEI as a public SKU, barcode payload, customer-facing URL, or event correlation key.
- Define secure correction procedures for mistaken identifier entry; preserve a traceable correction history.
- Restrict device ownership, activation-lock, and customer-linked evidence to authorized workflows.
- Establish deletion, retention, and legal-hold rules with appropriate legal advice before production.

## 8. Retention and disposal policy framework

No retention duration is asserted here. Management must approve a schedule by record class after reviewing operational, tax, accounting, warranty, consumer-protection, privacy, and litigation needs.

Schedule must address:
- product/SKU history;
- serialized-unit lifecycle and identifier records;
- receipts and acquisition evidence;
- movement ledger and adjustment approvals;
- reservations and order references;
- inspection, condition, and return evidence;
- transfer and custody records;
- audit and security events;
- exports and report artifacts;
- integration payloads and dead-letter records.

Controls:
1. Define retention start event and retention duration for each class.
2. Identify the system of record and accountable owner.
3. Define archival format, access, encryption, and retrieval expectations.
4. Suspend routine deletion for an approved legal/investigation hold.
5. Record disposal completion without retaining unnecessary deleted content.
6. Test retention and restoration procedures in non-production before activation.

## 9. Export and reporting governance

- Require a purpose and authorized role for confidential or restricted exports.
- Apply location scope and field minimization.
- Mask serial/IMEI unless full values are necessary and specifically authorized.
- Record requester, approver where required, filters, fields, timestamp, and export reference.
- Set expiry and secure storage rules for generated files.
- Avoid public or unrestricted share links.
- Do not send confidential inventory or valuation reports through unapproved personal email or storage.
- Clearly label projection, snapshot time, and reconciliation status.

## 10. Reconciliation, correction, and evidence preservation

- Reconcile physical counts, unit/lot state, movement history, reservations, and derived availability.
- Never repair discrepancies by deleting or editing historical movements.
- Use an approved compensating movement or adjustment workflow with evidence and authorization.
- Preserve original discrepancy, investigation, approval, and correction references.
- Separate suspected theft, damage, supplier shortage, data-entry error, and timing mismatch.
- Escalate unexplained or repeated variances according to severity.
- Retain enough evidence to reproduce the calculation and explain the final decision.

## 11. Incident and investigation controls

Potential triggers:
- unauthorized stock or permission change;
- repeated unexplained adjustment;
- duplicate or conflicting serialized identifier;
- suspected identifier exposure;
- unexplained ledger/availability mismatch;
- missing or altered audit evidence;
- suspicious bulk export;
- integration replay or service credential misuse.

Response framework:
1. Preserve relevant event, movement, access, and integration references.
2. Restrict affected access or pause the implicated automation where authorized.
3. Assign an incident owner and severity.
4. Record facts separately from assumptions.
5. Reconcile affected stock and customer/order impact.
6. Escalate to executive, legal, security, or insurer contacts when policy requires.
7. Document resolution, corrective action, and lessons learned.
8. Do not destroy or rewrite source evidence during investigation.

## 12. Governance metrics

Recommended control metrics:
- percentage of stock-affecting commands with complete audit evidence;
- privileged access reviews completed on schedule;
- restricted identifier reveal events;
- confidential export count and approval coverage;
- unresolved reconciliation discrepancies by age/severity;
- adjustments lacking required evidence (target zero);
- retention jobs and legal holds with recorded outcomes;
- integration dead-letter age and unresolved events;
- access removal timeliness after employee status changes.

Metrics must not encourage employees to suppress valid discrepancy reports or bypass required controls.

## 13. Acceptance checklist

- [ ] Data owner and classification exist for each inventory dataset and report.
- [ ] Stock-affecting actions preserve actor, time, reason, approval, and correlation evidence.
- [ ] Normal workflows cannot destructively rewrite movement history.
- [ ] Role and location permissions are enforced server-side.
- [ ] Full identifier access is restricted and audited.
- [ ] Cost/valuation and exports are restricted to approved roles.
- [ ] Retention schedule and legal-hold process are approved before activation.
- [ ] Export access, expiry, and storage are governed.
- [ ] Reconciliation corrections use approved compensating actions.
- [ ] Incident procedures preserve original evidence.
- [ ] Non-production tests verify authorization, masking, audit completeness, retention, and recovery.
